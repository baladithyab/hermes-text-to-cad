## TL;DR — what to adopt and why

For the user's goal — **correct, non-deformed, well-rendered models** — the highest-ROI adoptions, ranked:

1. **Topology/spec sidecar as machine-readable ground truth** (Section C). This is the single biggest lever: a versioned `<name>.topology.json` with counts, bbox, per-body data, and `valid` is what our numeric gate diffs and what feeds the vision gate. Without it, "correct" is unverifiable.
2. **Declared coordinate frame + named `features` list + `intent` string in `derive_spec`** (Section B). A declared frame (mm / XY base / +Z up / origin by part class) is *the* thing that prevents drift and deformation, and the `intent` guard stops over-delivery ("make a mug, not an elaborate vessel").
3. **Fillet/shell/loft/sweep safety wrappers in a first-party `cad_helpers.py`** (Section D). Oversized fillets, post-boolean face-selector breakage, and twisted lofts are the dominant *deformation* sources; clamping and validity assertions kill them at the source.
4. **GLB-as-source-of-truth PBR materials + auto-fit framing + studio lighting** (Section A). Faithful per-body color/metalness and deterministic 12%-padded framing make renders both pretty and *comparable* across reruns — essential for a diffing render gate.
5. **Compile/validate → smallest-repair loop with named failure classes** (Section B). Faithfulness comes from validated geometry; classified smallest-fix repair beats whole-model rewrites.

Everything below is reimplementable in pure Python in our subprocess-isolated `~/.venvs/cad` (cadquery / build123d / trimesh / vtk) and keeps `derive_spec`/`plan` LLM-free and unit-testable.

## Section A — Render quality (VTK render gate)

All citations are `file:line` into `/tmp/cad-refs/text-to-cad/skills/cad/scripts/cadpy_snapshot/` — the Python driver is `cadpy_snapshot/__main__.py`; the minified Three.js bundle is `cadpy_snapshot/runtime/snapshot-render.js`. The reference renders GLB in headless Chromium via Three.js; this studies the **approach to reproduce QUALITY in VTK**, not to copy code.

- **GLB-as-source-of-truth materials (PBR via GLTF).** The pipeline never authors materials in the renderer. The driver resolves a precomputed `.glb` artifact and refuses to render without it (`cadpy_snapshot/__main__.py:882-884`); the GLB carries per-body PBR metallic/roughness (and physical/clearcoat where present). The app reads back colors by traversing meshes keyed on `userData.cad` (`runtime/snapshot-render.js:4136` — `traverse(...userData.cad...)`), so each body keeps its assigned appearance. **Why:** faithful, deterministic per-body color and metalness/roughness without per-render guessing. **How (our stack):** export a GLB alongside the STEP, load with `vtkGLTFImporter` (imports meshes + PBR materials + scene graph in one call); fall back to `actor.GetProperty().SetInterpolationToPBR()` + `SetMetallic/SetRoughness/SetColor` per body when we synthesize materials ourselves.

- **ACES filmic tone mapping + sRGB output + tuned exposure.** Renderer is configured `outputColorSpace=SRGB`, `toneMapping=ACESFilmicToneMapping`, `toneMappingExposure` pulled from the theme's `lighting.toneMappingExposure` clamped to a floor (`snapshot-render.js:4136` — `outputColorSpace=at,toneMapping=nu,toneMappingExposure=Math.max(...,.05)`; alias `nu`→"ACESFilmic" confirmed at line 157). **Why:** the filmic curve prevents metal/specular blowout and gives product-shot contrast; correct color management avoids washed/dark PNGs. **How:** in VTK 9.3 the `vtkOpenGLRenderer.UseImageBasedLightingOn` path already does linear→sRGB; in-pipeline tone mapping is limited, so apply ACES + exposure as a post-pass on the rendered RGBA float buffer in numpy (in `~/.venvs/cad`, subprocess-isolated) before PNG encode.

- **Image-based lighting from an environment map (optional) + procedural gradient fallback.** When a theme enables `environment`, it loads an equirectangular image, sets `mapping=EquirectangularReflectionMapping`, `colorSpace=sRGB`, assigns it to `scene.environment` (IBL reflections) and optionally `scene.background` when `useAsBackground` (`snapshot-render.js:4136` — `environment||{};if(!n.enabled)...mapping=Hr...i.environment=a`; `Hr`→Equirectangular confirmed line 1). Backgrounds otherwise come from canvas radial/linear gradients (`createRadialGradient`/`createLinearGradient`). **Why:** IBL gives believable metal/plastic reflections that flat lights cannot; a soft gradient backdrop reads as a studio sweep. **How:** in VTK load an equirect HDR into a `vtkSkybox`/`vtkTexture`, call `renderer.UseImageBasedLightingOn()` and `renderer.SetEnvironmentTexture(tex)` (drives `vtkPBRIrradianceTexture`/prefiltered env automatically); for the gradient fallback use `renderer.GradientBackgroundOn()` with two colors, or a textured backdrop plane.

- **Studio multi-light rig with a soft-shadow key.** Theme `lighting` defines a hemisphere (sky+ground color), low ambient (~0.2), a directional key at roughly (160,-140,240) with `castShadow=true`, plus spot and point fills, each gated by `enabled` and overridable intensity/position (`snapshot-render.js:4136` — `directional?.intensity...position.set(160,-140,240),castShadow=!0`; `hemisphere?`, `ambient?.intensity,.2`, `spot?`, `point?`). Shadows use `shadowMap.enabled=true`, `shadowMap.type=PCFSoftShadowMap` (alias `tu`→PCFSoftShadowMap confirmed line 1). **Why:** 3-point lighting plus IBL gives shape-revealing highlights; soft PCF shadows ground the part without hard aliased edges. **How:** in VTK use three `vtkLight` instances (key/fill/back) with `SetPosition`/`SetIntensity`, approximate the hemisphere with an up-facing fill of distinct ground tint; enable shadows with a `vtkShadowMapPass` inside a `vtkRenderStepsPass`/`vtkSequencePass` pipeline (`renderer.SetPass(...)`).

- **Ground shadow-catcher plane.** A near-transparent plane sits just below the model (`receiveShadow=true`, `shadowOpacity~0.24`, positioned at model bottom minus a small epsilon) so only the contact shadow shows (`snapshot-render.js:4136` — `shadowOpacity,.24...receiveShadow=!0,i.add(f)`; `position.set(c.x,c.y,u-.03)`). **Why:** a contact shadow conveys scale and "sitting on a surface" without an opaque floor competing with the part. **How:** in VTK add a large `vtkPlaneSource` actor at the model's min-Z, make it a shadow receiver in the shadow pass, and set its material near-transparent so it only darkens where shadowed (or composite the shadow alpha in post).

- **Bounding-sphere auto-fit with fixed view directions and 12% padding.** Framing computes the model AABB → `center` and `radius=max(diag/2, minModelRadius)`, builds an orthonormal camera basis from a preset `direction`/`up`, and sizes an orthographic frustum half-extent from the projected bounds plus padding defaulting to **0.12** (`snapshot-render.js:4136` — `radius:Math.max(a.length()/2,n.minModelRadius)`; `padding...o=.12`; `i.top=d,i.bottom=-d,i.left=-d*aspect,i.right=d*aspect,i.near=.01,i.far=Math.max(radius*12,...)`). Presets are fixed unit vectors: iso `[1,-1,0.8]`, front `[0,-1,0]`, back `[0,1,0]`, right `[1,0,0]`, left `[-1,0,0]`, top `[0,0,1]`, bottom `[0,0,-1]` (`snapshot-render.js:4136`). **Why:** deterministic, repeatable framing across reruns and consistent part scale fills the frame — essential for a verification gate that diffs renders. **How:** in VTK compute bounds via `actor.GetBounds()`, set `camera.SetParallelProjection(True)`, place the camera along each preset direction at `center + dir*radius*k`, `camera.SetFocalPoint(center)`, `SetViewUp(up)`, then `camera.SetParallelScale(radius*(1+padding))` (or `renderer.ResetCamera(bounds)` then scale the parallel scale up by 12%). The same seven preset vectors can be reused verbatim — they are conventional view axes, not protected expression.

- **Selection focus/dim and highlight, not deletion.** Focusing parts fades the rest via `dimmedOpacity` (~0.035) and can recolor with `highlightColor`/`highlightOpacity` (`snapshot-render.js:4136` — `focusedPartIds...dimmedOpacity,s=.035...highlightColor`). **Why:** keeps spatial context while drawing the eye to the inspected body — better for review than hiding geometry. **How:** in VTK keep all GLTF actors loaded; for non-focused bodies set `property.SetOpacity(~0.04)` (requires depth peeling: `renderer.SetUseDepthPeeling(True)`), and tint focused bodies' `SetColor`/emissive.

- **Headless determinism knobs.** The renderer uses `preserveDrawingBuffer=true` (so the buffer survives until `toDataURL("image/png")`), `logarithmicDepthBuffer=true` (z-fighting on thin CAD walls), `antialias=true`, `device_scale_factor=1`, and a transparent clear (`setClearColor("#000000",0)`) when background is null (`snapshot-render.js:4136`; `__main__.py:1054-1057` viewport/DSF). Output sizes are profile-driven (diagnostic 1600×1200, presentation up to 2800×1800, orbit 960×640 — `__main__.py:45-62, 567-597`). **Why:** alpha PNGs, no depth artifacts, and fixed pixel sizes make renders reproducible and comparable. **How:** in VTK use `renderWindow.SetOffScreenRendering(1)`, `SetMultiSamples(8)`, `SetAlphaBitPlanes(1)` + `vtkWindowToImageFilter.SetInputBufferTypeToRGBA()` for transparency, and drive size from the same profile table.

**Net mapping:** GLB→`vtkGLTFImporter`; PBR→`SetInterpolationToPBR`; IBL→`UseImageBasedLightingOn`+`SetEnvironmentTexture`; 3-point→`vtkLight`×3; soft shadows→`vtkShadowMapPass`/`vtkRenderStepsPass`; ACES+sRGB→numpy post-pass; auto-fit→`ResetCamera`+parallel scale ×1.12 with the seven fixed direction vectors.

## Section B — Generation correctness (`derive_spec` + `core.plan`)

**Concern:** our `derive_spec` + `core.plan` under-specify **intent, coordinate frame, and placement** — the exact things both references treat as the source of faithful, non-deformed output.

- **Intent pass-through guard ("make a mug, not an elaborate vessel").** CADAM's parametric system prompt explicitly forbids rewriting/embellishing the user's request and bars adding unrelated constraints (`/tmp/cad-refs/CADAM/src/server/aiChat.ts:90`); the prompt-improver echoes the same "preserve its intent" rule (`prompt-generator.ts:42`, `aiChat.ts:216`). **Why:** embellishment is the #1 source of "that's not what I asked for." **How:** add a literal `intent` string to `derive_spec` (verbatim noun phrase + only the adjectives/dimensions the user gave) and surface it in `plan()` `notes` as a "do not add features absent from `intent`" instruction. The numeric gate already enforces *under*-delivery; this guards *over*-delivery.

- **"Never claim done unless the tool ran" self-check.** CADAM hard-binds the model: it may not say it created/updated/fixed a model unless `build_parametric_model` was called that turn, and a parametric user turn is forced into a structured tool path via `toolChoice: 'required'` (`aiChat.ts:88`, `:970-978`). **Why:** prevents hallucinated success with no artifact. **How:** our `core.plan`/`iterate` are LLM-free, but we can mirror the *principle* — `plan()` should emit a `claims_require` contract listing which assertions are only legitimate after a real tool result (e.g. "watertight/bbox/holes are UNVERIFIED until `cad_measure` returns" and "render exists only after `cad_render`"), so the agent can't report success on plan output alone.

- **Compile/validate → repair loop as a first-class step.** CADAM round-trips compilation back into the model and demands a fixing call on failure (`aiChat.ts:97-99`); earthtojake formalizes a classify→smallest-fix→rerun loop with named failure classes (`repair-loop.md:22-28`, classes at `:32-153`). **Why:** faithfulness comes from *validated* geometry, not first-shot codegen. **How:** extend `plan()` with a `repair_classes` field keyed to our gate failures (bbox/units mismatch → `repair-loop.md:81-96`; missing hole → `:98-112`; non-manifold/zero-thickness → `:49-64`; over-large fillet → `:66-79`) so `iterate` proposes the *smallest* responsible change, not a rewrite.

- **Enumerate features + coordinate frame + placement BEFORE code (the brief).** earthtojake writes a structured brief answering "what model / units / origin+base-plane+up-axis / overall dims / functional features / positioning datums / validation targets / assumptions" *before* any source (`natural-language-specs.md:18-46`, worked examples `:56-91`), with an explicit default frame (mm, XY base, +Z up, origin at center) at `SKILL.md:28-37`. **Why:** a declared frame is what prevents drift/deformation and lets validation be deterministic. **How:** enrich `derive_spec` with `units:"mm"`, `coordinate_frame:{origin, base_plane:"XY", up_axis:"+Z"}` (pick origin by part class — plate=footprint center, axisymmetric=rotational axis, enclosure=footprint center, per `positioning.md:63-70`), a **named** `features` list (each `{name, type, params, count}` rather than today's prose strings at `core.py:365-383`), and an `assumptions` list (only inferred choices, e.g. corner radius / clearance-hole standard, mirroring `natural-language-specs.md:64-65` and the M3/M4/M5 defaults at `SKILL.md:37`).

- **Symmetry / "derive, do not drift" placement.** earthtojake derives repeated-feature positions from count+pitch+radius+pattern axes and bans untraceable in-geometry constants (`parameters.md:74-78`, `positioning.md:77-89`). **Why:** hand-placed offsets cause asymmetry and self-intersection. **How:** add a `placement`/`symmetry` field to `derive_spec` — when N holes are detected, emit a derived symmetric pattern (e.g. `{pattern:"rect", count:N, mirror:["x","y"]}`) and have `plan()` print the `Locations(*positions)` idea so the agent puts every offset in a named parameter, never a literal.

- **Parameters as a contract with semantic names + bounds.** Both stacks insist on descriptive snake_case (no `w_r`), per-variable bounds/step, grouping, and clamping in code even when UI declares min/max (`aiChat.ts:130-144`, `parameters.md:38-68`). **Why:** bounds prevent impossible/self-intersecting states; readable names map intent→geometry. **How:** `plan()` should template each feature param as `name = default  # [min:step:max]` and require positive-dimension/manufacturable-wall bounds, so our exposed CadQuery params carry the same guardrails.

- **Higher-level primitives for organic/threaded geometry (anti-deformation).** CADAM routes threads to a screw library and organic/swept shapes to sweep/skin/bezier/rounding primitives instead of stacking cubes/cylinders, and raises facet count only for final renders (`aiChat.ts:108-128`). **Why:** hand-rolled helices and stacked primitives are the classic deformation/non-manifold trap. **How:** `plan()` should detect thread/organic keywords and steer to build123d sweeps/lofts/`fillet` + `trimesh` smoothing in `~/.venvs/cad`, and set a low preview tessellation with a high-res pass only for the export/vision gate.

- **Validate-then-report; say "not checked" honestly.** earthtojake reports only checks that actually ran and forbids implying a mate/alignment passed when unverified (`positioning.md:245-261`, `repair-loop.md:199-209`, `SKILL.md:98`). **Why:** honest provenance is faithfulness at the report layer. **How:** have `plan()`/`iterate` carry per-target validation status (`checked`/`not_checked`/`failed`) sourced from `cad_measure` and snapshot output, so our final response can only claim what the gate proved.

All techniques above are reimplementable in pure Python in our subprocess-isolated `~/.venvs/cad` (cadquery/build123d/trimesh/vtk) and keep `derive_spec`/`plan` LLM-free and unit-testable.

## Section C — Pipeline / contract (gen() → STEP → GLB → topology sidecar)

**Concern:** faithfully mirroring earthtojake/text-to-cad's gen() → STEP → GLB → topology-sidecar pipeline in our MIT / cadquery+trimesh stack.

- **Single-call generator returning live geometry, never file paths.** Their generator entrypoint is a zero-arg `gen_step()` that returns a build123d `Shape` (or an assembly list, or a small envelope dict with exactly one of `shape`/`instances`/`children`); a normalizer coerces all three forms into one internal envelope and *rejects* unknown keys and forbids any output-path field in the return value (`generation.py:747-774`, `step-generation.md:26,33-38`). **Why:** separating "produce geometry" from "decide where to write" makes the generator pure and re-runnable, lets the harness pick output paths, and makes the contract validatable (the exactly-one-content-field error is itself a faithfulness gate). **How:** define `gen() -> dict` returning `{"shape": <cadquery.Workplane|build123d Part>, "name": str}` and a normalizer that asserts a single geometry field, raises `TypeError` on extras, and never accepts a path. Load the user module via `importlib.util.spec_from_file_location` with a scrubbed `sys.path` restored in a `finally` (`generation.py:708-744`), run subprocess-isolated in `~/.venvs/cad`.

- **Source-hash provenance baked into every artifact.** Each generated STEP/topology artifact records `sourceKind:"python"` plus a hash of the generator source, so the harness can skip regeneration when source + mesh settings are unchanged (`generation.py:806,828-839,1394-1454`; reuse short-circuits at `:1528-1535`). **Why:** deterministic caching and a tamper-evident link from model→artifact (our numeric gate can refuse stale sidecars). **How:** `hashlib.sha256` of the generator file; embed `sourceKind`/`sourceHash` in the sidecar JSON and compare before re-meshing.

- **STEP written through an XCAF document carrying names + per-node colors.** They build an OCCT XCAF doc, walk the tree (`PreOrderIter`), set `TDataStd_Name` from each node's label, and apply colors via `SetColor(..., XCAFDoc_ColorSurf)`; for a colored leaf solid they explode to sub-shapes (SOLID/FACE/EDGE) so the color attaches to faces too, then write with `STEPCAFControl_Writer` with color/layer/name modes on and `write.precision.mode=AVERAGE` (`step_export.py:32-95,127-195`). **Why:** STEP that round-trips labels and colors is what makes downstream per-body materials and the vision gate meaningful. **How:** build123d 0.10 exposes the same OCP/XCAF writer — reuse build123d's `export_step` on a labeled `Compound`/`Part` whose children carry `.label` and `.color`; cadquery's assembly export also writes colored STEP. Keep names ASCII and inject a header originating-system tag for identity.

- **Hierarchical hand-rolled GLB writer that preserves XCAF colors, occurrence IDs, and dedups meshes/materials.** Rather than a naive tessellate-and-dump, they walk scene occurrences, resolve a color per node (explicit override → per-occurrence map → node color → prototype color → default), dedup materials by quantized RGBA key and meshes by a payload key, and emit nodes with a `matrix`, `mesh`, `extras.cadOccurrenceId`/`cadName`, and a CAD→GLB scale (`glb.py:411-458,690-826`). Materials are PBR with `metallic=0`, `roughness=0.55`, `doubleSided=true` (`glb.py:426-438`). A separate native-GLB path converts to Y-up/meter scale and strips CAD extras (`glb.py:105-126,247-281`). **Why:** per-body color and stable IDs make the render legible to the vision gate and let it reference specific bodies. **How:** in trimesh, tessellate each solid/body separately (OCCT triangulation via build123d, or `cadquery.exporters.export(..., "GLTF")`), build a `trimesh.Scene` with one named geometry per body, assign `trimesh.visual.material.PBRMaterial(baseColorFactor=..., metallicFactor=0, roughnessFactor=0.55, doubleSided=True)`, set node `extras` with our body id, and write `scene.export(file_type="glb")`. Provide a Y-up meter variant for external consumers.

- **Topology/spec sidecar as a versioned manifest with capabilities, bbox, stats, occurrence table, and edge classification.** The sidecar (`STEP_topology` v2) records `schemaVersion`, `sourceKind/Hash`, `stepHash`, `bbox`, `stats` (face/edge/solid/surface-half-edge counts), an `occurrences` row-table with declared `occurrenceColumns`, a `mesh` block (linear/angular deflection, resolution profile + hints), per-edge `flags`/`visibilityClass` classified by continuity (feature/tangent/seam/degenerate/boundary), and an `assembly` composition tree; it is stored either embedded in GLB bufferViews or as a sibling `topology.json` (`glb_topology.py:14-66,259-306`; index builder `glb.py:147-178`). **Why:** this is the machine-readable ground truth our **numeric gate** checks (body/solid count, bbox dims, named-feature presence) and what our **vision gate** correlates against the render (does the picture show the N bodies/colors the sidecar claims). **How:** write a sibling `<name>.topology.json`: `{schemaVersion, sourceKind, sourceHash, units:"mm", bbox:{min,max,size}, counts:{solids,faces,edges,vertices}, bodies:[{id,name,color,bbox,volume}], features:[{name,kind}], mesh:{linearDeflection,angularDeflection}, valid:bool}`. Populate counts/bbox/volume from build123d/OCP explorers; mark `valid` from `Shape.is_valid()`. The numeric gate diffs expected vs recorded counts/bbox; the vision gate consumes `bodies[].color/name` to caption the trimesh render.

- **Validation as deterministic-geometry-first, render-second.** Their docs make programmatic facts (bbox, labels, planes, positioning, measure/mate deltas) the source of truth and explicitly forbid using renders as a substitute, and forbid claiming tolerance/manufacturability without analysis (`inspection-and-validation.md:20-21,52-62,166-172`; `supported-exports.md:6-7`). **Why:** prevents a pretty GLB from passing a faithfulness check it shouldn't. **How:** our gate order = (1) STEP exists / non-empty + `is_valid`, (2) numeric sidecar diff, (3) trimesh GLB render → vision gate, with the numeric result authoritative when they disagree.

## Section D — Geometry vocabulary (`cad_helpers` API)

**Note on `parametricParts.ts`:** it is **not** a geometry-primitive catalog — it is a message-part narrowing module that parses AI-SDK UI message parts (`asParametricParts`, `getBuildParametricModelOutput` at `/tmp/cad-refs/CADAM/shared/parametricParts.ts:26,75`); the load-bearing artifact is just `{title, code}` (`:136-138`). CADAM's reusable geometry vocabulary is not in TypeScript at all: it is OpenSCAD source authored against external libraries (notably BOSL2) that are fetched and unzipped on demand only when the generated code references a library name (`/tmp/cad-refs/CADAM/src/worker/openSCAD.ts:325-369`). So "mirroring their parts" means re-deriving the *forms* (teardrop, loft, etc.) ourselves in build123d/cadquery, not copying their library.

- **Library-on-reference loading is informational only.** CADAM bundles primitives via a library registry and only injects a lib when the code mentions its name (`openSCAD.ts:325-330`). **Why:** confirms their primitives are an external BOSL2-style vocabulary, all-rights-reserved, so we must build our own. **How:** ship `scripts/cad_helpers.py` as first-party MIT helpers; never vendor BOSL2.

- **Manifold/lazy-union solid-validity backend.** They force `--backend=manifold --enable=lazy-union` and detect the "not a 3D object" failure to fall back to 2D SVG (`openSCAD.ts:190-220`). **Why:** a non-manifold or accidentally-2D result is the #1 cause of deformed/empty renders. **How:** every helper returns a watertight `cadquery.Solid`/build123d `Solid`; wrap output in a `trimesh.is_watertight` + `is_winding_consistent` assertion in the subprocess (`~/.venvs/cad`), and if a boolean leaves a degenerate shell, raise rather than emit a broken mesh.

- **Profiles-first, builder-context discipline.** paths→sketches→solids→labels (`build123d-modeling.md:96-98,111`). **Why:** organic forms deform when you skip the closed-wire stage. **How:** our `loft`/`sweep`/`revolve` helpers take fully-closed 2D profile factories, validating each wire is closed before lofting (build123d `BuildSketch`/`make_face`), matching the "open sketch → invalid face" failure mode (`:171`).

- **Loft between aligned profiles, ruled vs smooth.** build123d `loft` between ≥2 coplanar sketches on parameterized planes (`:111`). **Why:** profiles with mismatched vertex counts or twisted orientation produce pinched/twisted lofts. **How:** `loft_profiles(...)` accepts ordered `(z, profile)` pairs on parallel planes, normalizes start-vertex/winding, and exposes a `ruled: bool` to choose linear vs smooth interpolation; returns a `Solid`.

- **Sweep along path with constant section.** `sweep profile along path` (`build123d-modeling.md:128`). **Why:** sweeps deform when the section isn't kept normal to the path or the path has too-tight curvature. **How:** `sweep_along(section, path, keep_normal=True)` builds the path in a `BuildLine`, sweeps the closed section, and guards minimum radius-of-curvature ≥ section half-width.

- **Revolve of a closed profile about an axis.** `revolved part → revolve profile` (`:128`). **Why:** a profile crossing the axis self-intersects. **How:** `revolve_profile(profile, axis=Axis.Z, degrees=360)` asserts every profile point is on one side of the axis; returns a `Solid`.

- **Fillet-safe wrapper (radius vs local geometry).** Their top failure is "fillet radius larger than local edge geometry" and "face selector changes after a boolean" (`:169,172`). **Why:** oversized or post-boolean fillets are the dominant deformation/crash source. **How:** `safe_fillet(part, edge_selector, radius, fallback_ratio=0.4)` selects edges by axis/normal/position (never list index, per `:133-142`), clamps radius to a fraction of the shortest adjacent edge length, tries the fillet, and on OCC failure retries at reduced radius before giving up — returns the original solid if no safe radius exists, with a flag.

- **Shelling for hollow enclosures.** `hollow enclosure → shell` (`:126`). **Why:** shelling a face whose adjacent walls are thinner than the offset yields self-intersecting inner shells. **How:** `shell_solid(part, open_faces, wall)` validates `wall < 0.5 * min_wall_span` before offsetting inward.

- **Flush emblem via boolean co-planarity, not floating geometry.** positioning's `--mode flush` / "delta 0.00 mm" coplanar contact idea (`positioning.md:185-191,222`). **Why:** emblems that float or z-fight render deformed. **How:** `flush_emblem(host, emblem_profile, face, depth, mode="raised"|"engraved")` locates the emblem sketch exactly on the host face plane and fuses (raised) or cuts (engraved) so faces are coplanar by construction; returns the combined `Solid`.

- **Disc-in-ring button via concentric named-datum offsets.** Derived from parameterized concentric placement and symmetric signs (`positioning.md:75-89,239`). **Why:** a disc that isn't truly coaxial with its ring reads as deformed. **How:** `disc_in_ring_button(ring_od, ring_id, disc_d, height, gap)` builds ring and disc on the same axis (`Axis.Z`), enforces `disc_d < ring_id - 2*gap`, returns a labeled `Compound` (ring + disc) so the parts stay independently inspectable.

- **Teardrop (printability) profile.** Not in their TS, but the canonical self-supporting hole/peg form. **How:** `teardrop(radius, angle=45, length, axis)` constructs a circle unioned with a triangular apex (chamfered top so overhangs stay under the print angle), extrudes/sweeps along `axis`; returns a `Solid` suitable for horizontal holes that print without supports.

- **Parameters-first, centered origins.** Every dimension a named variable; symmetric parts origin-at-center (`build123d-modeling.md:54-65`, `positioning.md:63-69`). **Why:** faithful, reproducible, mate-checkable geometry. **How:** all helpers take explicit float params (no magic constants), default origins to part center, label outputs, and are callable from a subprocess-isolated `gen_step()`-style entrypoint validated via measure/frame deltas.
